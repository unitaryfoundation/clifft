    Partner Effects from Leakage and Loss¶

    When a two-qubit gate touches a leaked or lost qubit, Clifft skips the gate. By default, a computational partner qubit is left untouched. On real hardware, however, the drive pulses for that gate can still disturb the computational partner or cause it to leak as well.

    noncomp.PartnerEffect models this disturbance as single-qubit Pauli noise and optional leakage spreading on the computational partner. The originally leaked or lost qubit keeps its status, while any spread leakage persists and can affect later gates and measurements in the same shot.

    Start with Leakage and Loss for the five-level model, classifiers, and sampling API.

    Choose where effects apply¶

    Need Configuration
    The same effect across physical two-qubit gates Model(gate_partner_effects={...})
    A different effect for a specific gate or operand direction Model(interactions=[InteractionRule(...)])
    An effect at an individual circuit line LEAKAGE_INTERACTION(...) or LOSS_INTERACTION(...)

    gate_partner_effects and interactions attach automatically to physical two-qubit gates in the circuit. LEAKAGE_INTERACTION and LOSS_INTERACTION are explicit circuit instructions that run where you place them, in addition to any model-configured gate effects.

    Model-wide defaults¶

    Pass gate_partner_effects to noncomp.Model with keys "leaked" (matching either LEAK_G or LEAK_E) and/or "lost". Each default applies regardless of whether the leaked or lost qubit is the first or second operand of a two-qubit gate, whenever the other operand is computational. Omitted keys leave the partner unchanged.

    This example fully depolarizes the computational partner whenever a two-qubit gate touches a leaked qubit, while leaving lost-qubit encounters as no-ops:

    from clifft import noncomp

model = noncomp.Model(
    classifier=noncomp.Classifier(
        [[1, 0, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5], [0, 1, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5]]
    ),
    gate_partner_effects={
        "leaked": noncomp.PartnerEffect(pauli=(0.25, 0.25, 0.25)),
    },
)
result = noncomp.sample("LEAKAGE(1) 0\nCX 0 1\nM 1", model, shots=4096, seed=1, final_status=True)
assert abs(result.measurements[:, 0].mean() - 0.5) < 0.04
assert (result.final_status[:, 0] == noncomp.QubitStatus.LEAK_G).all()
assert (result.final_status[:, 1] == noncomp.QubitStatus.COMPUTATIONAL).all()

    To use Clifft's compile-once fixed execution, two-qubit gates that can pair a leaked or lost qubit with a computational partner must configure full partner depolarization (pauli=(0.25, 0.25, 0.25) with spread_probability=0).

    Probability conventions¶

    PartnerEffect takes two optional keyword arguments:

    Argument Meaning Default
    pauli=(px, py, pz) Mutually exclusive X, Y, and Z error probabilities on the partner (0, 0, 0)
    spread_probability=p Probability that the partner leaks after the Pauli step 0

    px + py + pz must be at most 1, leaving 1 - px - py - pz as the no-error probability. spread_probability is an independent draw in [0, 1] applied after the Pauli channel, so a partner can receive a Pauli error and then leak on the same gate.

    For full single-qubit depolarization (equal I, X, Y, and Z probabilities), pass pauli=(0.25, 0.25, 0.25). In Stim's DEPOLARIZE1(p) parameterization that corresponds to p=0.75 (more generally, DEPOLARIZE1(p) corresponds to pauli=(p/3, p/3, p/3)).

    Leakage spreading applies the same g -> leak_g, e -> leak_e jump as LEAKAGE(p), evaluated on the partner's state after the Pauli channel rather than copying the source qubit's leaked level. The source qubit remains leaked, and only "leaked" effects support spread_probability > 0 ("lost" effects with nonzero spreading raise ValueError).

    Specific rules replace defaults¶

    Use InteractionRule when a particular gate or operand direction behaves differently from gate_partner_effects. A matching rule replaces the model-wide default for that (gate, source_operand, source_status) combination rather than merging fields, so effect=PartnerEffect() disables the default for that case:

    from clifft import noncomp

model = noncomp.Model(
    gate_partner_effects={
        "leaked": noncomp.PartnerEffect(pauli=(0.25, 0.25, 0.25)),
    },
    interactions=[
        noncomp.InteractionRule(
            gate="CX",
            source_operand=0,
            source_status="leaked",
            effect=noncomp.PartnerEffect(),
        ),
        noncomp.InteractionRule(
            gate="CX",
            source_operand=1,
            source_status="leaked",
            effect=noncomp.PartnerEffect(
                pauli=(0.01, 0, 0.1), spread_probability=0.02
            ),
        ),
    ],
)

    source_operand is the operand index within each two-qubit pair (0 for the first qubit, 1 for the second), not a qubit ID. On CX 2 5, source_operand=0 matches when control qubit 2 is leaked and target qubit 5 is computational; source_operand=1 matches the reverse. Symmetric gates such as CZ use the same written operand order (0 and 1).

    Gate aliases share rules: "CX" and "CNOT" refer to the same gate, so defining rules for both with the same source_operand and source_status raises ValueError.

    Supported gates¶

    Defaults and rules apply to physical two-qubit unitary gates, including CX, CY, CZ, swap gates, square-root Pauli-pair gates, and parameterized R_XX, R_YY, and R_ZZ rotations (across all angles).

    They do not apply to:

    • single-qubit gates, measurements, noise instructions, or identity no-ops;
    • classical feedback such as CX rec[-1] 0 (both operands must be physical qubits);
    • multi-target Pauli-product gates such as SPP and R_PAULI, even when a product contains two qubits; or
    • composite gates that decompose during parsing (CH, CCX, CCZ). Rules cannot name CH, CCX, or CCZ directly, and instead apply to the elementary two-qubit gates produced by their decomposition. If decomposition choice affects your hardware noise model, write the circuit using the target hardware's native two-qubit gates.

    A swap gate touching a leaked or lost qubit is skipped like any other two-qubit gate and then applies any configured partner effect; it does not move the leaked or lost state to the other site.

    Explicit circuit annotations¶

    Use LEAKAGE_INTERACTION and LOSS_INTERACTION directly in a circuit to apply a partner effect at an individual position (with or without a preceding gate):

    LEAKAGE_INTERACTION(px, py, pz, spread_probability) source partner
LOSS_INTERACTION(px, py, pz) source partner

    LEAKAGE_INTERACTION fires when source is leaked and partner is computational; LOSS_INTERACTION fires when source is lost and partner is computational. All probability arguments are required, and each (source, partner) pair must name two distinct plain qubit indices. Multiple pairs on one line run left to right, so leakage spreading in one pair can affect a later pair on the same line.

    from clifft import noncomp

model = noncomp.Model(
    classifier=noncomp.Classifier([[1, 0, 1, 0, 0], [0, 1, 0, 1, 1]])
)
result = noncomp.sample(
    """
    LEAKAGE(1) 0
    LOSS(1) 2
    LEAKAGE_INTERACTION(1, 0, 0, 1) 0 1
    LOSS_INTERACTION(1, 0, 0) 2 3
    HERALD_LEAKAGE_EVENT 0 1
    M 1 3
    """,
    model,
    shots=16,
    seed=2,
    final_status=True,
)
assert result.measurements.all()
assert (result.final_status[:, 1] == noncomp.QubitStatus.LEAK_E).all()
assert (result.final_status[:, 3] == noncomp.QubitStatus.COMPUTATIONAL).all()

    Here the certain X error flips qubit 1 from g to e before the certain leakage jump, leaving it in LEAK_E. Qubit 3 receives an X error and stays computational. Interaction instructions append no measurement records (the four bits in result.measurements come from HERALD_LEAKAGE_EVENT 0 1 and M 1 3).

    Standalone LEAKAGE_INTERACTION (with spread_probability=0) and LOSS_INTERACTION instructions support arbitrary (px, py, pz) probabilities in compile-once fixed execution. Like LEAKAGE and LOSS, these instructions require clifft.noncomp; ordinary clifft.compile rejects them.

    Ordering with level-transition hooks¶

    Model partner effects check whether an operand was already leaked or lost entering a two-qubit gate, whereas gate transition hooks (Model(transitions={"CX": ...})) model leakage or loss caused by that gate. Around each physical two-qubit gate, Clifft therefore evaluates:

    1. The gate or its model partner effect: if both operands are computational, the gate runs normally; if one operand is leaked or lost and the other is computational, the gate is skipped and the matching partner effect runs (Pauli noise first, then leakage spreading); if both operands are noncomputational, the gate is skipped with no computational partner to disturb.
    2. Automatic gate transition hooks (transitions={"CX": ...}), in operand order. Hooks still run when the gate was skipped, and see any new leakage caused by step 1.
    3. Subsequent instructions written on later lines of the circuit.

    Explicit interaction instructions run after a gate's transition hooks

    Writing LEAKAGE_INTERACTION on the line after CX 2 5 places it at step 3, after any transitions={"CX": T} hooks on qubits 2 and 5:

    CX 2 5
# automatic LEVEL_TRANSITION[CX] hooks on 2 and 5 run here
LEAKAGE_INTERACTION(0.25, 0.25, 0.25, 0) 2 5

    That handwritten instruction can react to leakage just created by the CX hook, whereas a model-configured gate_partner_effects or InteractionRule on CX runs at step 1 and reacts only to leakage that existed before CX 2 5.

    Control transition placement explicitly¶

    When you need explicit control over ordering at a single circuit site, give the transition a custom non-gate name (such as "after_cx") and write LEVEL_TRANSITION[after_cx] and LEAKAGE_INTERACTION in the order you want:

    import numpy as np
from clifft import noncomp

transition = np.zeros((5, 5))
transition[noncomp.Level.LEAK_G, noncomp.Level.G] = 1
model = noncomp.Model(
    transitions={"after_cx": transition.tolist()},
    classifier=noncomp.Classifier([[1, 0, 1, 0, 0], [0, 1, 0, 1, 1]]),
)

before = """
X 1
CX 0 1
LEAKAGE_INTERACTION(1, 0, 0, 0) 0 1
LEVEL_TRANSITION[after_cx] 0 1
M 1
"""
after = """
X 1
CX 0 1
LEVEL_TRANSITION[after_cx] 0 1
LEAKAGE_INTERACTION(1, 0, 0, 0) 0 1
M 1
"""
assert noncomp.sample(before, model, shots=16, seed=3).measurements.all()
assert not noncomp.sample(after, model, shots=16, seed=3).measurements.any()

    In before, both qubits are still computational when LEAKAGE_INTERACTION runs, so qubit 1 stays in 1. In after, LEVEL_TRANSITION[after_cx] leaks qubit 0 first, so LEAKAGE_INTERACTION flips qubit 1 to 0. To get the before-transition order automatically across every CX, configure gate_partner_effects or an InteractionRule on the model instead.

    Mixing explicit and generated effects¶

    Explicit LEAKAGE_INTERACTION and LOSS_INTERACTION instructions do not override gate_partner_effects or InteractionRule entries. If both are present at the same gate, their effects apply in sequence: for example, a model-configured X error of 0.2 followed by an explicit X error of 0.3 gives a net X flip probability of 0.38 while the partner stays computational (or no second effect if the first step leaked the partner). Avoid configuring the same physical disturbance through both mechanisms.

    Multi-qubit noise vs. partner effects¶

    gate_partner_effects and InteractionRule attach only to physical two-qubit unitary gates, not to noise instructions. Ordinary noise instructions keep their standard behavior when a target is leaked or lost:

    Noise instruction Behavior on a leaked or lost target
    DEPOLARIZE2/3, PAULI_CHANNEL_2/3 Skipped for the entire pair or triple, including computational partners
    E / CORRELATED_ERROR and ELSE_CORRELATED_ERROR Chain probabilities stay intact; Pauli terms act on computational targets and do nothing on leaked or lost targets
    Single-qubit noise (DEPOLARIZE1, X_ERROR, ...) Skipped on leaked or lost targets; applied as usual on computational targets

    Two-qubit gate noise such as DEPOLARIZE2 placed after CX 0 1 is therefore skipped whenever qubit 0 or 1 is leaked or lost; Clifft does not automatically convert it into single-qubit noise on the surviving partner. Use gate_partner_effects or InteractionRule to model how a skipped two-qubit gate disturbs its computational partner.

    Note the difference between PAULI_CHANNEL_2 and CORRELATED_ERROR: E(p) X0 X1 still flips computational qubit 1 when qubit 0 is leaked, whereas an XX term inside PAULI_CHANNEL_2 on 0 1 is skipped altogether. Two noise encodings that are equivalent on ordinary qubits can therefore behave differently once leakage or loss occurs.
