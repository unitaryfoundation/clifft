    Leakage and Loss¶

    Pauli noise acts within a qubit's two-dimensional computational subspace. Real hardware can instead drive the state out of that subspace through leakage, or lose the physical carrier from its site entirely through loss. Neither process is a Pauli channel, so the site no longer holds an ordinary qubit state.

    Leakage and loss can persist until the qubit relaxes, the carrier is recaptured, or the site is reset. One event can therefore affect a sequence of later gates, measurements, and detector outcomes. Clifft tracks each site's status alongside its ordinary coherent simulation. A computational site remains in the quantum state and can be in a superposition or entangled; a leaked or lost site occupies one definite noncomputational level in each shot.

    Use clifft.noncomp.sample() to simulate a circuit and model in one call, or clifft.noncomp.compile() to compile them once for repeated sampling.

    This page walks through the clifft.noncomp API. Related pages cover:

    • Partner Interactions: Pauli noise and leakage spreading on computational qubits paired with a leaked or lost qubit.
    • Noncomputational States: mathematical definition of the five-level model, quantum-jump back-action, and fixed execution.
    • Tutorials: Delayed Loss in a Surface Code (introductory) and Logical Shor Noise Sweep (full five-level neutral-atom model).

    A model and its outputs¶

    A noncomp.Model defines how qubit sites enter, leave, and are observed outside the computational subspace. It contains an initial level distribution, transition matrices, a measurement classifier, optional partner interaction effects, and policies such as whether a reset restores a lost site. The classifier maps a known level to probabilities for a binary measurement result and, optionally, a herald.

    Clifft models five Level values: the computational levels g and e, identified with \(\lvert 0\rangle\) and \(\lvert 1\rangle\); the leaked levels leak_g and leak_e; and lost. These levels index transition-matrix rows and columns and classifier probability columns.

    QubitStatus describes a site's status in a sampled shot. Both g and e correspond to COMPUTATIONAL because the site remains in the coherent quantum state rather than occupying a known basis level. The statuses LEAK_G, LEAK_E, and LOST name definite noncomputational levels. An initial weight on g or e prepares the corresponding computational basis state; later evolution may put the site in a superposition or entangle it with other sites.

    noncomp.sample returns the usual measurements, detectors, and observables arrays, plus a heralds array for each measurement slot. To also record each qubit's end-of-shot QubitStatus, pass final_status=True (by default, result.final_status is None).

    A minimal model¶

    This example randomizes the initial state across all five levels and shows how the classifier maps those levels to measurement outputs. It has no transitions between levels after sampling begins.

    import numpy as np

from clifft import noncomp

Level = noncomp.Level

# Classifier P[symbol][level]: g and leak_g read 0, e and leak_e read 1,
# lost heralds (third symbol).
classifier = noncomp.Classifier(
    [
        [1, 0, 1, 0, 0],  # P(record 0 | level)
        [0, 1, 0, 1, 0],  # P(record 1 | level)
        [0, 0, 0, 0, 1],  # P(herald   | level)
    ]
)

model = noncomp.Model(
    initial_state=[0.92, 0.03, 0.02, 0.01, 0.02],  # P(g, e, leak_g, leak_e, lost)
    classifier=classifier,
)

r = noncomp.sample("M 0", model, shots=20_000, seed=1, final_status=True)

# final_status: (shots, num_qubits) of QubitStatus values.
status = r.final_status[:, 0]
lost = (status == noncomp.QubitStatus.LOST).mean()
assert abs(lost - 0.02) < 0.005

# heralds: (shots, num_measurements); 1 where the classifier heralded.
assert (r.heralds[:, 0] == (status == noncomp.QubitStatus.LOST)).all()

# symbols() reports the herald as value 2 instead of the placeholder bit.
symbols = r.symbols()
assert np.array_equal(symbols[:, 0] == 2, r.heralds[:, 0])

    The output arrays have the same layout as ordinary sampling: every measurement still occupies its record slot. On a computational site, Clifft performs the requested quantum measurement. For M and MR, the classifier's g and e columns can then add computational-basis readout confusion. On a leaked or lost site, the classifier supplies the result in place of a quantum measurement and applies to M, MX, and MY alike.

    Computational readout confusion is Z-basis only

    The classifier's g and e columns apply to M and MR. On a computational site, MX, MY, MRX, and MRY perform their ordinary quantum measurements without consulting those columns. Use measurement noise such as MX(p) or MY(p) for readout errors in another basis. If the site is leaked or lost, the classifier supplies the result regardless of measurement basis.

    When the classifier emits its herald symbol, heralds marks the slot and the binary measurements entry holds a uniformly drawn placeholder. Detectors and observables use that binary bit as usual; the herald remains separate. symbols() folds the herald back in as a third value per slot (0, 1, or 2) for comparison with tools that report loss in-band.

    heralds[shot, slot] means that the classifier emitted its third symbol at that measurement slot. It does not identify when or where the underlying transition occurred, so it is not an exact spacetime erasure flag. The herald is side information for downstream analysis or adaptive decoding.

    Omitting initial_state starts every site in g, which matches the standard Clifft convention that all qubits start in \(\lvert 0 \rangle\). A model capable of leakage or loss requires a classifier whenever the circuit contains any physical-qubit measurement, even if the measured site cannot itself become noncomputational. MPAD and the status checks below are exempt because they do not perform a physical qubit measurement.

    Nondestructive status checks¶

    HERALD_LEAKAGE_EVENT and HERALD_LOSS_EVENT report the status at a circuit position without changing the site's quantum state or occupation. Each plain qubit target appends one ordinary record bit, in target order:

    Status HERALD_LEAKAGE_EVENT HERALD_LOSS_EVENT
    Computational 0 0
    LEAK_G or LEAK_E 1 0
    LOST 0 1

    These instructions take no probability arguments and accept plain qubit indices (no ! target inversion). Their record bits work with DETECTOR, OBSERVABLE_INCLUDE, and classical feedback (rec[...]), require no classifier, and leave result.heralds set to 0 at those slots. Each check reports whether the qubit is leaked or lost at that point in the circuit, not when the jump happened.

    from clifft import noncomp

result = noncomp.sample(
    """
    LEAKAGE(1) 0
    LOSS(1) 1
    HERALD_LEAKAGE_EVENT 0 1
    HERALD_LOSS_EVENT 0 1
    CX rec[-1] 2
    DETECTOR rec[-4]
    OBSERVABLE_INCLUDE(0) rec[-1]
    """,
    noncomp.Model(),
    shots=16,
    seed=1,
)
assert (result.measurements == [1, 0, 0, 1]).all()
assert result.detectors.all()
assert result.observables.all()
assert not result.heralds.any()

    Status checks are noiseless by default. To model detection errors, apply READOUT_NOISE to the resulting record bit. For example, a 2% false-positive rate and a 5% false-negative rate:

    HERALD_LEAKAGE_EVENT 0
READOUT_NOISE(0.02, 0.05) rec[-1]

    Use READOUT_NOISE(0, p) for missed detections only, or READOUT_NOISE(p) for symmetric errors. READOUT_NOISE flips only the recorded bit; the qubit's quantum state and status are unchanged.

    Like transition annotations, status checks require clifft.noncomp; ordinary clifft.compile rejects them.

    Transitions: hooks and inline annotations¶

    A transition matrix T[to][from] gives the probability of a transition event from one source level to one destination level. The destination may equal the source; a column's deficit below 1 is the no-jump probability.

    Transitions act as noise associated with specific circuit positions. There are four ways to place them:

    • Gate hooks. Give a transitions entry the name of a physical gate, such as "CZ" or "S", to apply that matrix to each operand after every occurrence of the gate. Noise instructions, annotations, identity no-ops, and composite instructions that expand during parsing (MXX/MYY/MZZ, CH/CCX/CCZ) cannot be hooked and raise ValueError.
    • Inline references. Write LEVEL_TRANSITION[name] 0 in the circuit to apply a named matrix from Model(transitions={...}) to qubit 0 at that specific line. Use this for custom transition names (such as "idle_decay") or one-off placements.
    • Inline leakage. Write LEAKAGE(p) 0 in the circuit to move g -> leak_g and e -> leak_e with probability p without defining a 5x5 matrix. An already leaked or lost qubit is unchanged.
    • Inline loss. Write LOSS(p) 0 in the circuit to move any occupied level to lost with probability p without defining a 5x5 matrix.

    Gate hooks are shorthand for inline references. Before sampling, Clifft expands each hook into a LEVEL_TRANSITION[name] annotation for every physical site operand of the hooked gate. Classical feedback (CX rec[-1] 0) receives no annotation: a record-conditioned Pauli is a frame update, not a physical execution of the hooked gate.

    A transition back to g or e can represent relaxation or recapture into the computational subspace; a reset represents active re-preparation.

    Combining hooks and inline annotations¶

    This example applies one transition after every CZ, reuses the same matrix at one explicitly selected position, and adds an independent inline loss.

    from clifft import noncomp

Level = noncomp.Level


def T(entries):
    """Build a 5x5 transition matrix T[to][from] from {(to, from): p}."""
    m = [[0.0] * 5 for _ in range(5)]
    for (to, frm), p in entries.items():
        m[to][frm] = p
    return m


classifier = noncomp.Classifier([[1, 0, 1, 0, 0], [0, 1, 0, 1, 1]])
leak = T({(Level.LEAK_E, Level.G): 0.01, (Level.LEAK_E, Level.E): 0.01})

model = noncomp.Model(
    transitions={
        "CZ": leak,  # hook: evaluated on each site after every CZ
        "manual_leak": leak,  # evaluated only where explicitly referenced
    },
    classifier=classifier,
)

circuit = """
    H 0
    CZ 0 1
    LEAKAGE(0.01) 0
    LOSS(0.005) 1
    LEVEL_TRANSITION[manual_leak] 0
    M 0 1
"""
r = noncomp.sample(circuit, model, shots=1000, seed=7)
assert r.measurements.shape == (1000, 2)

    What happens on a leaked or lost site¶

    Most unitary gates, ordinary noise channels, and classical corrections that touch a leaked or lost site are skipped as a whole. By default, this leaves every computational partner unchanged. For physical two-qubit unitary gates, gate_partner_effects and InteractionRule can replace this identity with Pauli noise and optional leakage spreading on the computational partner. Leaked and lost sources are configured separately. See Partner Interactions for the configuration API, explicit circuit annotations, and their ordering with transition hooks. A single-qubit measurement is different: it keeps its record position, but the classifier rather than the measurement basis supplies its result (M, MX, and MY alike).

    Correlated-error instructions (E/CORRELATED_ERROR and ELSE_CORRELATED_ERROR) are the exception. Clifft retains them to preserve chain conditioning; their Paulis still act on computational operands but are inert on a noncomputational site.

    Loss from an entangled pair¶

    Losing one half of an entangled pair leaves the partner in the reduced state. For a Bell pair, that reduced state is maximally mixed, so its computational-basis measurement is unbiased:

    import numpy as np

from clifft import noncomp

Level = noncomp.Level

lose_all = [[0.0] * 5 for _ in range(5)]
lose_all[Level.LOST][Level.G] = 1.0
lose_all[Level.LOST][Level.E] = 1.0

model = noncomp.Model(
    transitions={"S": lose_all},  # the hooked S loses its qubit with certainty
    classifier=noncomp.Classifier([[1, 0, 1, 0, 0], [0, 1, 0, 1, 1]]),
)

r = noncomp.sample("H 0\nCX 0 1\nS 0\nM 0\nM 1", model, shots=20_000, seed=2, final_status=True)

survivor = r.measurements[:, 1]
assert abs(survivor.mean() - 0.5) < 0.01  # the survivor's Z readout is unbiased
assert (r.final_status[:, 0] == noncomp.QubitStatus.LOST).all()
assert (r.final_status[:, 1] == noncomp.QubitStatus.COMPUTATIONAL).all()

    Classifier results feed detectors¶

    The classifier supplies the binary record bit before detectors and observables are evaluated, so a noncomputational readout can produce detector events:

    import numpy as np

from clifft import noncomp

Level = noncomp.Level

leak_up = [[0.0] * 5 for _ in range(5)]
leak_up[Level.LEAK_E][Level.G] = 1.0
leak_up[Level.LEAK_E][Level.E] = 1.0

model = noncomp.Model(
    transitions={"S": leak_up},
    classifier=noncomp.Classifier([[1, 0, 1, 0, 0], [0, 1, 0, 1, 1]]),  # leak_e reads 1
)

# A reference measurement, then the leaked one; the detector XORs them.
circuit = "M 1\nH 0\nS 0\nM 0\nDETECTOR rec[-1] rec[-2]"
r = noncomp.sample(circuit, model, shots=1000, seed=4)
assert (r.detectors[:, 0] == 1).all()  # the leaked qubit always reads 1

    State-dependent rates resolve at runtime¶

    If a transition's rate differs between g and e, the jump probability for a computational site in a superposition depends on the amplitudes at that point in the shot. The draw happens at sample time against the live state, so the statistics are exact. In the example below, the e component of \(\lvert + \rangle\) contributes probability \(\tfrac12\) to reading 1. The g component has weight \(\tfrac12\), and a fraction \(p\) of it leaks to a level that also reads 1. The total is therefore \(\tfrac12 + \tfrac{p}{2}\).

    from clifft import noncomp

Level = noncomp.Level

p = 0.4
leak_from_g = [[0.0] * 5 for _ in range(5)]
leak_from_g[Level.LEAK_E][Level.G] = p

model = noncomp.Model(
    transitions={"S": leak_from_g},
    classifier=noncomp.Classifier([[1, 0, 1, 0, 0], [0, 1, 0, 1, 1]]),
)

r = noncomp.sample("H 0\nS 0\nM 0", model, shots=20_000, seed=5)
assert abs(r.measurements[:, 0].mean() - (0.5 + p / 2)) < 0.01

    Evaluating a transition against the quantum state at that point also preserves correlations. When the destination depends on the source level, the leaked site's classified readout stays correlated with its entangled partner. The semantics and active-width cost are described in Noncomputational States.

    Model policies¶

    Two noncomp.Model options control how resets and state-dependent transition rates behave:

    • reset_restores_lost (default False): whether reset instructions (R, RX, RY, and the reset half of MR, MRX, MRY) re-prepare a lost site into the computational subspace. Resets always restore a leaked site, and measure-and-reset instructions always record a classified measurement bit even when the reset half is dropped.
    • damping (default "exact"): how Clifft handles the no-jump state update when g and e have different total jump rates (\(p_g \neq p_e\)). When a qubit in superposition survives a transition without jumping, its state shifts slightly toward the less leaky level. "exact" simulates that shift accurately, but a transition may need one additional active coordinate, increasing active width by one and doubling the \(2^k\) active-state size. Transitions reuse existing active coordinates when possible. Gates such as SWAP can move the affected state elsewhere, so damping the same physical qubit again can add more width. "neglect" skips the no-jump update to avoid that extra width, introducing an error of order \(\lvert p_g - p_e \rvert\) at each transition. When \(p_g = p_e\), as in LEAKAGE(p) and LOSS(p), there is no state bias and both settings are exact.

    Compiling and execution¶

    noncomp.sample(circuit, model, ...) compiles and samples in a single call. When running multiple shot counts, seeds, or worker settings against the same circuit and model, compile once with noncomp.compile() and reuse the returned NonComputationalProgram:

    from clifft import noncomp

model = noncomp.Model(
    classifier=noncomp.Classifier([[1, 0, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5], [0, 1, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5]]),
    gate_partner_effects={
        "leaked": noncomp.PartnerEffect(pauli=(0.25, 0.25, 0.25)),
    },
)
program = noncomp.compile("H 0\nLEAKAGE(0.01) 0\nCX 0 1\nM 0 1", model)
assert program.strategy == "fixed"

result = program.sample(shots=10_000, seed=42)
assert result.final_status is None
second = program.sample(shots=10_000, seed=43, threads=2)

    The compiled program stores a copy of the circuit and model and can be sampled repeatedly, including concurrently from multiple threads. noncomp.sample() accepts all of the compile() and sample() options below and compiles on every call.

    Fixed execution vs. trajectories¶

    Clifft has two execution strategies for leakage and loss, with identical model and output semantics:

    • Fixed execution (strategy == "fixed"): compiles the quantum circuit once up front and can process shots in batches, including circuits with non-Clifford gates.
    • Trajectory execution (strategy == "trajectory"): supports the full five-level model by stepping through one shot at a time and recompiling the remaining circuit whenever a jump changes later operations.

    By default (execution="auto"), noncomp.compile() selects an exact supported fixed plan when possible and uses trajectories otherwise. The table summarizes the main eligibility checks; operation support and the statuses that can occur at each circuit position also matter.

    Requirement for "fixed" Uses "trajectory" when...
    All qubits start in g (initial_state omitted or [1, 0, 0, 0, 0]) initial_state puts nonzero probability on e, leak_g, leak_e, or lost
    Transitions use inline LEAKAGE(p), LOSS(p), or all-zero named matrices A nonzero named transition matrix is invoked, explicitly or through a gate hook; unused matrices do not prevent fixed execution
    No leakage spreading can occur A partner effect with spread_probability > 0 can encounter a leaked source and a computational partner; unreachable effects do not prevent fixed execution
    Full partner depolarization PartnerEffect(pauli=(0.25, 0.25, 0.25)) whenever a two-qubit gate can pair a leaked or lost qubit with a computational qubit Such a pairing uses the default no-op (drop) or partial Pauli noise; when both operands are noncomputational, there is no computational partner to depolarize
    Classifier treats leak_g and leak_e identically at every leaked measurement Classifier assigns different probabilities to leak_g and leak_e on a qubit that can be leaked when measured
    final_status=False, or resets/loss clear any leaked state before circuit end final_status=True and a qubit could end the circuit leaked (since final_status distinguishes LEAK_G from LEAK_E)
    Compiled fixed program fits within max_active_width Fixed program exceeds max_active_width (Clifft falls back to trajectories, where each compiled continuation must still fit within max_active_width)

    Other operations can also require trajectories. For example, multi-qubit Pauli-product gates such as SPP, TPP, and R_PAULI do not support fixed execution when a target can be leaked or lost, even with full partner depolarization configured. Use program.explain() to inspect the selected strategy and any fallback reason.

    Why two-qubit gates need full partner depolarization for fixed execution

    Fixed execution keeps two-qubit gates in the compiled quantum circuit even when one qubit has leaked or been lost. That produces the exact same result as dropping the gate only when the computational partner is completely depolarized (pauli=(0.25, 0.25, 0.25)). Standalone LEAKAGE_INTERACTION and LOSS_INTERACTION instructions do not attach to a two-qubit gate and support arbitrary Pauli probabilities in fixed execution. See Fixed execution for the derivation.

    Invalid circuits or models always raise ValueError during compile(); automatic fallback applies only to valid requests outside the fixed subset.

    Compilation and sampling options¶

    Options passed to noncomp.compile() (and forwarded by noncomp.sample()):

    Argument Default Meaning
    final_status False When True, populate result.final_status with per-qubit QubitStatus values.
    execution "auto" "auto" selects "fixed" when eligible and "trajectory" otherwise; "fixed" raises ValueError if ineligible; "trajectory" forces trajectories.
    max_active_width None Cap on peak active width. Under "auto", an over-cap fixed program falls back to trajectories; trajectory continuations exceeding the cap raise during sampling.

    Options passed to program.sample() (and noncomp.sample()):

    Argument Default Meaning
    shots (required) Number of shots to sample.
    seed None Integer seed for reproducible results, or None for hardware entropy.
    threads 1 Number of cross-shot CPU workers, or "auto" for hardware concurrency. Memory use grows with worker count.
    batch_size "auto" Batching mode ("auto", "tune", 1, or N > 1); see below.
    tuning_budget_seconds None Soft time limit in seconds for batch_size="tune" trials (defaults to 0.25 s).
    collect_stats False Populate result.stats with a NonComputationalStats execution report.

    batch_size controls how many shots a worker processes together:

    • "auto" (default): choose a batch size automatically; trajectories run one shot at a time.
    • "tune": try several batch sizes to find a faster setting. Available only for fixed execution.
    • 1: process shots individually. Fixed execution still reuses its compiled program.
    • N (N > 1): request up to N shots per batch. Available only for fixed execution.

    Leave batch_size="auto" for normal use. Clifft chooses whether to process shots individually or in batches based on your circuit, shot count, and memory needs, without spending time on trial runs. For repeated sampling of the same circuit, batch_size="tune" can help find a faster setting, as shown below.

    Changing the batch size can change individual samples, even with the same seed. With the same program, seed, shot count, and batch size, changing threads does not change the results.

    Post-selected survivor sampling, fixed-fault importance sampling, intra-shot thread layouts, and custom optimization pass managers are not supported by clifft.noncomp.

    Tuning batch size¶

    For fixed execution, batch_size="tune" tries several batch sizes and chooses one based on the estimated time to produce your requested samples. Tuning adds time to the call, so reuse the chosen batch size on later calls to benefit without repeating the trials:

    result = program.sample(10_000, seed=42, batch_size="tune", tuning_budget_seconds=0.25)
# Reuse the chosen batch size without repeating the trials.
next_result = program.sample(10_000, seed=43, batch_size=result.batch_tuning.batch_size)

    tuning_budget_seconds sets a soft time limit on the trial runs (defaulting to 0.25 seconds), and result.batch_tuning records the chosen batch_size and timings. Trial shots are discarded and use a separate random stream, so they never enter your returned results. Because live timings can pick a different batch size on another run, pass the numeric batch_size explicitly when you need exact seeded replay. See Budgeted batch calibration for more on how batch tuning works across Clifft.

    Inspecting execution¶

    Call program.explain() to check which strategy was chosen, see the circuit line and reason if fixed execution fell back to trajectories, and view peak active width estimates without running any shots. To see what happened during a sampling run (such as how many shots ran in batches versus one at a time, how many workers were used, and how often trajectory execution recompiled), pass collect_stats=True:

    print(program.explain())

result = program.sample(shots=10_000, seed=42, collect_stats=True)
assert result.stats is not None
assert result.stats.packed_shots == 10_000
assert result.stats.continuation_compilations == 0

    See NonComputationalStats for all counter, memory, and timing fields.

    Trajectory continuations¶

    When program.strategy == "trajectory", noncomp.compile() validates the circuit and expands model hooks once, while quantum compilation still happens during sample(). Whenever a jump changes which later operations apply, the worker compiles a continuation for the remaining circuit and resumes the shot (result.stats.continuation_compilations counts how many occurred).

    To resume mid-shot without disturbing earlier measurement correlations, trajectory execution uses a restricted internal compiler pipeline. See Optimization Passes for the pass restrictions and Noncomputational States for how continuations compose with Clifft's simulator.

    Limits¶

    • Leakage and loss do not move between sites. Partner effects can apply Pauli noise or spread leakage to a computational partner, but the original leaked qubit stays leaked (and SWAP gates touching a leaked or lost site are dropped rather than moving it). Processes where one qubit relaxes while transferring its leakage to a neighbor are not supported.
    • Multi-qubit Pauli noise channels drop as a whole. DEPOLARIZE2/3 and PAULI_CHANNEL_2/3 touching a leaked or lost site are skipped completely, whereas CORRELATED_ERROR / ELSE_CORRELATED_ERROR chains still apply Pauli terms to their remaining computational targets. Noise encodings that are equivalent on ordinary qubits can therefore behave differently after leakage or loss; see Multi-qubit noise vs. partner effects.
    • Leakage is incoherent. Jumps out of the computational subspace produce definite classical occupations (leak_g, leak_e, or lost), not coherent superpositions between computational and leaked levels.
    • MPP is not supported when the model can leak or lose qubits, because multi-qubit parity has no well-defined single-bit classifier outcome once an operand leaves the qubit subspace. Decompose parity checks into explicit ancilla gates and single-qubit measurements instead.
    • EXP_VAL is not supported. Circuits containing EXP_VAL are rejected before sampling because clifft.noncomp does not compute expectation values.
    • Physical measurements require a classifier whenever the model can produce leakage or loss, even if the measured qubit itself never leaks or is lost in the circuit.
