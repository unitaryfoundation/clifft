# Conditional Clifford reduction investigation

This is a research record, not an execution-architecture specification. The
repository's planning, allocation, and correctness invariants continue to apply.

## Baseline and question

The branch `codex/bt27-fault-specialization` was created from this chat's checkout
at `3eed09f693418e8471f3ad81b1639e7797a31491`, then fast-forwarded to the reviewed
[PR 552](https://github.com/unitaryfoundation/clifft/pull/552) baseline,
`ed2a125ad7daf75deef7e21d66af2a4af4a366bf`.

PR 552 tracks symbolic measurement signs but exposes only fixed constraints to
ordinary rewrites. Private Pauli-fault signs are eliminated by intersection.
Analysis atoms describe physical outcomes and must not be confused with the
planner's coordinate-dependent symbols. This investigation does not change
those contracts.

The immediate question is whether fixing Pauli-fault realizations lets ordinary
compilation find small complete executions of BT27 while measurements and
feedback remain live. That separates three problems:

1. Finding a reduced action once the faults are known.
2. Executing branch-dependent corrections without excessive active width.
3. Sharing analysis and execution plans without expensive per-realization work.

Successful specialization is evidence for the first question only. Failed
specialization does not prove intrinsically large non-Clifford width.

## Workload details that matter

- Use `tests/fixtures/merlin_bt27.stim`, generated by Merlin revision
  `097380fac1a3968ca47925146e211fe990f4c396`, without inverse target scoring.
- The workload has three 27-qubit data blocks and nine decoded logical qubits,
  plus preparation ancillas. The logical outputs are at
  `0, 1, 2, 27, 28, 29, 54, 55, 56`. Three logical qubits is the per-block count.
- The pinned noisy benchmark inserts `DEPOLARIZE1(0.001)` on data qubits 0-80
  immediately before the first T gate. It is a single noise layer, not a full
  circuit-level noise model with interleaved faults.
- PR 552 reports peak width 28 -> 9 for noiseless BT27 and a modified Z-only
  diagnostic, but 28 -> 28 for the original depolarizing layer.
- Noiseless BT81 separately encounters an 87-variable region beyond 64-bit
  phase parity masks. Widening that algebra is not a solution to BT27's noise
  gap by itself.

## Fault-specialization experiment

Replace the depolarizing layer with actual deterministic X, Y, and Z gates at
the same location. Keep physical measurement outcomes, recorded outcomes,
feedback, detectors, observables, and any later noise live. Do not use
probability-one noise instructions: the fixed-constraint analysis deliberately
also preserves the no-fault reference for those instructions.

Compute expected detector and observable parities once from the original
noise-free circuit and pass them explicitly to each specialized program.
Never normalize a fault against a newly computed faulty reference. Do not
postselect, retry, or omit difficult fault patterns.

The initial matrix is:

- the unspecialized depolarizing circuit as a negative control;
- the no-fault circuit;
- all 243 single-Pauli faults in the 81-qubit noise layer;
- reproducible examples at several multiple-fault weights;
- categorical samples from the original noise law, including no-fault samples.

Stratified cases diagnose the compiler and are not a probability-weighted
logical-error estimate. The ordinary sampled cases retain their multiplicities.

Record original and optimized T counts, whole-program planned peak width,
phase-pass collection counters, timing, and the first operation exceeding the
nine-logical-qubit width when it occurs. Inspect high-width controls without
allocating a dense execution state. Execute bounded-width cases, preserving all
measurement records. Keep programs sequentially for this diagnostic; it does
not establish the memory cost of retaining a cache or prepared batch.

Validate the harness and physical semantics against independent references.
Existing Aer and Stim BT27 tests cover the noiseless and Z-only cases. Broader
X/Y and multiple-fault validation must be identified explicitly rather than
implied by agreement on compilation widths or detector counts alone.

## Decisions after the diagnostic

- If known faults admit small complete executions, identify precisely which
  Clifford corrections and coordinate interfaces vary. Equal widths, equal
  instruction counts, or equal printed plans are not compatibility proofs.
- Compare fixed planned actions, bounded additional width, and explicit
  continuation using common certificates. General continuation requires its
  own architectural review before implementation.
- Add an explicitly labeled interleaved-noise stress test. The current phase
  collector stops at faults anticommuting with a collected axis or a required
  constraint; retaining entry signs alone does not remove that barrier.
- Compare with existing Merlin on matched supported outputs, including noisy
  code switching. Do not require a new Clifft DCP backend for that comparison.
- Keep growing-output experiments separate: exact factored cores and restricted
  DCP storage are candidates to compare, not an imposed implementation order.
- Shared-reference coherent DCP sums remain exploratory. Efficient pairwise
  overlaps do not bound term growth or establish a complete update contract.

## Scientific and resource limits

The experiment must preserve conditional quantum states and classical record
semantics, not only acceptance rates. The initial probes are not a decoder
study, a logical-error-rate estimate, a reproduction of the published protocol,
or a guarantee of efficient arbitrary-noise simulation. Report preparation and
sampling separately, and do not present coefficient bytes as total peak memory.

## Initial sweep on 2026-10-08

The pinned compiler was rebuilt in this branch's development environment. The
driver retains the 126 visible measurement records and 72 detectors. The base
fixture has no observable columns; quantum-state probes are added separately
for validation. No postselection or inverse target scoring is used.

| Unspecialized depolarizing control | 1 | 28 | 216 |
| No fault | 1 | 9 | 37 |
| Every single X, Y, or Z fault | 243 | 9 | 37 |
| Two faults | 8 | 9 | 37 |

There are 292 unique specialized patterns. Of the 128 ordinary draws, 119 are
fault-free and nine are distinct single faults already in the exhaustive
single-fault set. Their compilation measurements reuse those patterns; the
draws are not 128 newly compiled plans or a production cache experiment.

The median single-fault compile time is about 63.8 ms, excluding width
inspection. Sampling 256 shots takes about 1.22 ms, including executor
preparation, with one worker and `batch_size=1`. Each pattern has two timing
trials after a common warmup. These are exploratory local timings, not a
controlled performance comparison between implementations. Recompiling once
per trajectory would plainly dominate the subsequent execution on this input.

The sweep took about 38.6 seconds. Its process high-water RSS was 43,448 KiB
(about 42.4 MiB), including Python and diagnostic data with programs retained
sequentially. It excludes the separate reference-validation process and says
nothing about keeping every executable resident at once. The width-28 control
was inspected but never allocated or sampled.

The unspecialized control reports three capped blocks out of four examined,
with no accepted expansion or rewrite. The first optimized operation taking
width above nine is a T_DAG at HIR index 645. Lost entry constraints also enlarge
the collection problem; this control does not isolate the variable ceiling
from the missing conditional facts.

### A concrete execution difference

Comparing the no-fault circuit with X on qubit 0 gives identical sequences of
37 optimized T/T_DAG operations. Nevertheless, some downstream measurement
Pauli bodies change, and four executable actions change from `RECORD_CLASSICAL`
to `MEASURE_DORMANT` (records 78, 107, 108, and 84). Both programs have peak width
nine and 298 executable actions. The no-fault program has 96 classical-record
actions and 93 dormant measurements; X0 has 92 and 97 respectively.

This is a concrete reason not to infer executable compatibility from width,
T count, or a common non-Clifford core. The next investigation should identify
the varying measurement and frame actions and determine which can be planned
together. It need not begin by implementing general continuation.

### Reproduction

Install the repository development dependencies and build this revision. Run:

```bash
.venv/bin/python tools/profile/profile_bt27_fault_specialization.py \
  --output /tmp/bt27-fault-specialization.json

# Optional independent reference, from the pinned Merlin checkout:
uv pip install --no-deps /path/to/merlin
.venv/bin/python tools/profile/validate_bt27_fault_specialization.py \
  --study /tmp/bt27-fault-specialization.json \
  --output /tmp/bt27-fault-validation.json

.venv/bin/python -m pytest tests/python/test_external_feedback.py -q
```

The default sweep includes eight examples at each selected multiple-fault
weight, 128 original-noise draws, two timing trials, and 256 shots per trial.
Raw sweep data is retained in [bt27-specialization.json](bt27-specialization.json).
The existing Aer/Stim tests pass: 17 passed and eight unavailable GPU cases
skipped.

### Independent validation

All 292 specialized patterns passed comparison with Merlin built from the pinned
revision. Each simulator executed 512 shots per pattern with distinct seeds.
The checks cover 95 logical Pauli probes: all single-qubit X/Y/Z expectations,
all two-qubit XX expectations, and 32 reproducible higher-weight Pauli probes.
They also compare 1,592 joint moment features per pattern, including raw record
and detector parities, probe second moments, and probe/classical correlations.
The largest statistical discrepancy score was 3.456, below the diagnostic's
six-standard-error threshold with its finite-sample allowance.

For observed syndrome patterns shared by both samples, the validator also
compares conditional probe values when at least eight shots from each simulator
show constant values within the group. This checked 2,790 groups across 252
patterns, including every single-fault pattern. The largest conditional-probe
difference was approximately 1.53e-16. Dense-fault cases with insufficient
matching syndrome samples receive only the joint-moment checks.

Merlin automatically computes reference parities from each supplied circuit.
Those references would include the inserted deterministic faults. The validator
therefore reconstructs raw detector and observable parities from measurement
records using Stim's measurement-to-detector converter with reference sampling
disabled. Clifft's detector bits are checked against these raw parities and the
original fixed reference. The reference simulator's normalized detector bits
are not used in the comparison.

The validation took about 422 seconds and is retained in
[bt27-validation.json](bt27-validation.json), including the input study hash and
reference binary hash. These finite probes and statistical checks are not full
tomography, a proof for untested faults, or a complete scientific workflow with
a decoder. They provide independent evidence beyond T counts, widths, or
acceptance-only checks. Repository-wide pre-commit checks also pass.
