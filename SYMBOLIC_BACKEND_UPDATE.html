Architecture

What changed?

Legacy Clifft

Pauli localization → SVM bytecode → localized operations

Predictable flat execution, but localization can add dense transformations.

Symbolic Clifft

symbolic frame → shared coordinate plan → direct kernels

The planner fixes topology once; each shot resolves signs and applies direct active-state operations.

SymFT

Techniques introduced

  • Symbolic signs for noise, feedback, and branches.
  • Adaptive coordinates for the active state.
  • Direct Pauli kernels without localization.
  • Packed shots and product components for additional throughput.

Ablation findings

What drives performance?

  • Removing per-shot Clifford work is the base win.
  • Batching is strongest at low k and fades as dense work grows.
  • Layout and SIMD kernels dominate larger active states.
  • Product components are narrow but powerful for weakly coupled high-k states.

Status

Done and next

Implemented

  • Broad CPU feature parity behind the experimental API.
  • Incremental affine-expression registers.
  • Direct and fused scalar kernels plus AVX-512 paths.
  • Existing Python quantum-mechanics tests reused across backends.

Next measurements

  • Same-Pauli and dynamic-sign fusion.
  • Remaining rotation and measurement kernels.
  • Planner and continuation compilation.
  • AVX2, then OpenMP, batching, and product components.

Will legacy go away?

That is the goal: preserve the Python API and replace its implementation after correctness, portability, compilation, and remaining surfaces are ready.

Why no new SVM?

The SVM itself was not a measured performance driver. A compact tape may still become an architectural or inspection boundary later.

What about GPU?

Revisit the design around the symbolic plan, with a GPU-specific lowered program, state layout, and batching strategy.
